Skillet corn with paprika & vinegar

Get rid of the extra water first, then add a little oil and finish with a bright splash of vinegar.

Read the recipe ↓

You'll need

  • About 12 oz frozen corn
  • A little cooking oil (amount still being tested)
  • Paprika, garlic powder and salt, to taste
  • A splash of vinegar

How to make it

  1. Add frozen corn to a wide, dry skillet over medium to medium-high heat. Cook uncovered until the water mostly evaporates.
  2. Add a little oil and cook, stirring occasionally, until heated through and lightly golden in places.
  3. Season with paprika, garlic powder and salt.
  4. Finish with vinegar, taste and adjust.

Test note: Delicious on the first try, but a bit too oily. Next time: measure the oil and try less.

Everyday magic

Pan-browned mixed vegetables

Frozen peas, carrots, corn, and friends get a better texture when you cook off their moisture before adding oil.

Read the recipe ↓

You'll need

  • About 12 oz frozen mixed vegetables
  • A little cooking oil (amount still being tested)
  • Garlic powder, onion powder, salt and pepper
  • Optional: a squeeze of lemon or splash of vinegar

How to make it

  1. Cook the frozen vegetables uncovered in a wide, dry skillet over medium to medium-high heat until most moisture evaporates.
  2. Add a little oil and continue cooking until the vegetables pick up some color.
  3. Season to taste with garlic powder, onion powder, salt and pepper.
  4. Optionally finish with a little lemon or vinegar.

Test note: The texture worked well; the flavor could be bolder. Next trial: measure the seasonings and test different finishes.

The reason behind the recipes

Why frozen veggies?

I think eating more vegetables should be easy, affordable, and genuinely enjoyable.

Frozen vegetables are a great starting point: they're inexpensive, last a long time, and don't require much planning. But there's a difference between vegetables that are merely convenient and vegetables you're excited to eat.

I'm experimenting with small changes that make a big difference—and sharing what works, what doesn't, and what I'm still figuring out.

Think of this as a little public kitchen notebook. You're welcome to cook along!

A little cooking science

Why cook frozen veggies dry first?

Frozen vegetables release water as they heat. Letting that water evaporate before adding oil makes it easier to get a less soggy texture and a little browning.

It's a tiny change that can make a familiar side dish more satisfying.

💧 First: let the water escape.Use a wide pan and leave it uncovered.
🔥 Then: add a little oil.Once the pan is less watery, the vegetables can begin to brown.
🍋 Last: wake up the flavor.A little acid or seasoning can make a simple vegetable dish pop.
